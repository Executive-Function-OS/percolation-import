                               II. GROUNDS FOR STAY
15

16
     1. The underlying 2019 foreclosure judgment is void. The certified Register of

17
       Actions for Case No. 17CV38591 shows that the case was dismissed via a General

18
       Judgment of Dismissal on July 26, 2019. The General Judgment of Foreclosure

19
       was signed four days later, on July 30, 2019, when the court lacked subject matter

20
       jurisdiction. The case was not reinstated until July 31, 2019. Under Bailey v.

21
       Vanderkin, 198 Or App 232, 237, 108 P3d 68 (2005), a judgment signed after

22
       final dismissal is void ab initio. This jurisdictional fact was not before the Court

23
       when it ruled on April 29, 2026.

24

25
     2. A void judgment is a legal nullity that cannot be reformed, enforced, or given any

26
       effect. See Citizens Savings & Loan Ass’n v. McDonald, 191 Or App 45 (2003).

27
       Because the proposed General Judgment in this action purports to validate and

28
       reform a void 2019 judgment, its entry would create a recorded court order resting

       on a jurisdictional nullity, permanently clouding title to Plaintiff’s real property.
 1
         3. The balance of equities strongly favors a stay. Plaintiff faces irreparable harm
 2
            to her property rights if a judgment validating a void foreclosure is entered of
 3
            record. Defendants, who seek to enforce a judgment signed when the court lacked
 4
            jurisdiction, will suffer no cognizable harm from a brief delay. See Krein v. Szewc,
 5
            287 Or App 481, 490, 403 P3d 520 (2017) (injunctive relief is appropriate to
 6
            avoid requiring plaintiff to sue repeatedly for damages where legal remedy is
 7
            inadequate).
 8

 9
         4. Plaintiff is finalizing a motion under ORCP 71 B(1)(d) to set aside the void 2019
10
            foreclosure judgment on grounds of lack of subject matter jurisdiction. That
11
            motion will be filed promptly.
12

            1. OBJECTION: PROPOSED JUDGMENT VIOLATES UTCR 5.100

 5
     Defendants’ UTCR 5.100 Certificate of Readiness is facially defective. Counsel checked
 6
     box 3(c), stating Plaintiff “agreed to independently file any remaining objection,” but
 7
     failed to comply with the mandatory requirements of UTCR 5.100(3)(b). Having received
 8
     Plaintiff’s objections, counsel was required to file a copy of those objections with the
 9
     court and indicate which remain unresolved.
10

11
     The failure to file Plaintiff’s objections renders the certificate non compliant and the
12
     proposed judgment not ready for signature. The UTCR 5.100 process is the mechanism
13
     by which parties preserve error regarding the form of a judgment. Dudrov v. State, 274 Or
14
     App 636, 361 P3d 647 (2015).
15

16

17
        2. OBJECTION: COURT LACKS JURISDICTION TO ENTER PROPOSED
18
                                          JUDGMENT
19

20
     The proposed judgment purports to reform a General Judgment of Foreclosure from Case

21
     No. 17CV38591. That judgment was signed on July 30, 2019. However, the certified

22
     Register of Actions for Case No. 17CV38591 confirms the case was dismissed and closed

23
     on July 26, 2019.

24

25
     Under Bailey v. Vanderkin, 198 Or App 232, 237 (2005), a judgment signed after a case

26
     has been dismissed is void for lack of subject matter jurisdiction. A void judgment is a

27
     legal nullity and cannot be reformed, corrected, or given legal effect. This Court lacks

28
     jurisdiction to grant relief based upon a void instrument. See Citizens Savings & Loan

     Ass'n v. McDonald, 191 Or App 45 (2003) (void judgment must be vacated); Burt &
 1
     Gordon v. Stein, 128 Or App 350 (1994) (a void judgment cannot be cured by waiver or
 2
     reformed).
 3

 4

 5
        3. OBJECTION: NUNC PRO TUNC CANNOT CURE A VOID JUDGMENT
 6

 7
     The proposed judgment improperly seeks to correct the void 2019 judgment nunc pro

 8
     tunc. The purpose of a nunc pro tunc order is to supply an omission in the record of an

 9
     action that was actually taken; it cannot be used to retroactively create authority or

10
     jurisdiction where none existed. State v. Lavitsky, 171 Or App 506 (2000). Because the

11
     court lacked jurisdiction on July 30, 2019, any judgment signed on that date was a nullity,

12
     and there is no prior valid judicial act to which a nunc pro tunc order can relate back.

13
     Equitable reformation cannot cure this jurisdictional defect.

14

15

16
